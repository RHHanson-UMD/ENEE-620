Gaussian conditional estimation.

Move the correlation and observation to see why the best mean-square estimate is affine, why the error is independent of the observation, and when a Gaussian covariance becomes singular.

02 / EXPLAIN

Why the formulas work

STEP 1 / THE BEST ESTIMATE

Conditional expectation is MMSE

For any square-integrable X, its conditional expectation minimizes mean-square error among functions of Y. Joint Gaussianity makes that optimal estimator affine.

E[X | Y = y] = ρ (σX / σY) y

Here both means are zero. With nonzero means, add μX and replace y by (y − μY).

STEP 2 / THE ERROR

Uncorrelated becomes independent

Let e = X − E[X | Y]. The orthogonality principle gives E[eY] = 0. Because e and Y are jointly Gaussian, zero covariance implies independence.

e ⟂ Y (independent for this Gaussian pair)

For arbitrary non-Gaussian variables, zero correlation alone does not establish independence.

STEP 3 / THE DISTRIBUTION

Conditioning shrinks uncertainty

The error's distribution does not depend on Y. Consequently, the conditional law of X given Y = y is Gaussian with mean equal to the estimator and fixed variance.

Var(X | Y = y) = σX² (1 − ρ²)

This is also the minimum achievable mean-square error.

STEP 4 / THE BOUNDARY

When covariance is singular

The joint covariance determinant is σX² σY² (1 − ρ²). At |ρ| = 1, X is an affine function of Y, the conditional variance is zero, and the joint pair has no ordinary two-dimensional density.

det(R) = σX² σY² (1 − ρ²)

Try the two endpoints of the correlation slider to observe this limiting case.

03 / PRACTICE

Predict before you slide

  1. Set ρ = 0. What happens to the estimate after changing y? Why?
  2. Set ρ = −0.8. Does a positive observed Y raise or lower the estimate of X?
  3. Move |ρ| toward 1. How do the error spread and covariance determinant change?
  4. Why is the sample error correlation not a mathematical proof of independence?
Show reasoning and answers

At ρ = 0, E[X | Y = y] = 0 because jointly Gaussian and uncorrelated implies independence. For negative ρ, positive y decreases the estimate. As |ρ| approaches 1, conditional variance tends to zero and the determinant vanishes. Sample correlation is only a finite-sample statistic; the proof uses orthogonality plus joint Gaussianity.
